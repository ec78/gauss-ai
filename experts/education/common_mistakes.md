# Common Mistakes

Common AI and user failure modes the Education Expert should help agents avoid.

- Writing a content list instead of a learning sequence.
- Confusing documentation with education (`EXPERT.md`, Relationship to Documentation Expert).
- Overloading beginners too early, ignoring cognitive load (`BODY_OF_KNOWLEDGE.md`, Cognitive Load).
- Missing prerequisites, so a learner starts a topic without what they need.
- Vague learning objectives that can't actually be assessed.
- Exercises that do not match the stated objectives.
- No feedback or assessment plan.
- No remediation path for a learner who doesn't succeed the first time.
- Unsupported GAUSS syntax in examples.
- Unsupported statistical claims in lessons.
- Treating a clear explanation as durable learning — explaining something once is not the same as a learner retaining and being able to apply it.
- Treating product onboarding as the same as education (`EXPERT.md`, Relationship to future Product Expert).
- Making lessons too broad to actually teach anything well.
- Failing to address misconceptions explicitly.
- Skipping practice in favor of explanation alone.
- Claiming syntax correctness from lesson quality — a pedagogically excellent tutorial can still contain invalid GAUSS syntax (`EXPERT.md`, Relationship to GAUSS Language Expert).
- Claiming statistical validity from teaching clarity — a well-taught explanation of a method does not confirm the method is appropriate (`EXPERT.md`, Relationship to Econometrics Expert).
- Claiming product value from training quality — good training can support adoption but does not prove a feature is valuable or should be prioritized (`EXPERT.md`, Relationship to future Product Expert).
