# v0.16.0 Education Expert Foundation Plan

## Release Goal

`v0.16.0` introduces the Education Expert as the fifth operational expert. The Code Assistant currently has implementation correctness through the GAUSS Language Expert, methodological correctness through the Econometrics Expert, maintainability/design correctness through the Software Engineering Expert, and documentation/explanation correctness through the Documentation Expert. None of the four owns learning design as such: whether a piece of content is sequenced, scaffolded, and practiced in a way that actually helps a learner build durable understanding over time — independent of whether the underlying code is syntactically valid, the underlying method is methodologically sound, the underlying design is maintainable, or a single piece of content is clearly documented. The Education Expert closes that gap:

- **GAUSS Language Expert** — implementation correctness.
- **Econometrics Expert** — methodological correctness.
- **Software Engineering Expert** — maintainability/design correctness.
- **Documentation Expert** — documentation/explanation correctness.
- **Education Expert** — learning objectives, sequencing, scaffolding, practice, feedback, assessment, and learner progression.

This remains documentation/evaluation-driven expert routing, exactly like the four experts before it: an operational profile, activation triggers, decision rules, and manually-evaluated benchmarks — not a runtime orchestration engine. No code parses or enforces any of this at runtime; a response is checked against it manually, the same way task-mode fit is checked today. Adding a fifth expert changes the *shape* of routing (more combinations to document and evaluate) but not its *mechanism*.

## Expert Boundary Rules

- GAUSS-specific syntax claims must defer to the GAUSS Language Expert and verified evidence — the Education Expert never asserts exact GAUSS syntax on its own authority.
- Econometric/statistical claims must defer to the Econometrics Expert — the Education Expert never asserts model appropriateness or causal validity on its own authority.
- Code design/refactoring claims must defer to the Software Engineering Expert — the Education Expert never asserts maintainability on its own authority.
- Documentation/reference clarity claims must defer to the Documentation Expert — the Education Expert never asserts reference-page quality as its own primary authority.
- The Education Expert may sequence and scaffold concepts but cannot invent GAUSS syntax to fill an exercise or example.
- The Education Expert may design learning exercises around statistical concepts but cannot certify methodology.
- The Education Expert may create practice/reflection activities around code design but cannot certify maintainability.
- The Education Expert may recommend supporting docs but cannot claim documentation quality as its primary authority — that remains the Documentation Expert's claim.
- The Education Expert is distinct from the Documentation Expert:
  - **Documentation** helps a user complete a task or understand a reference right now.
  - **Education** helps a user learn over time through sequencing, scaffolding, exercises, feedback, and pedagogy.
- The Education Expert is distinct from the (future) Product Expert:
  - **Education** supports learner development — building durable understanding and skill.
  - **Product** supports user problem framing, workflow fit, adoption, positioning, and prioritization.
  - A well-designed training curriculum improving comprehension or adoption is never evidence that a feature is valuable, product-market-fit is established, or a feature should be prioritized.
- If multiple experts activate, implementation, methodology, maintainability, documentation, and education claims must remain separate and separately attributable in the response, extending the same coordination principle `v0.9.0`, `v0.14.0`, and `v0.15.0` established for two, three, and four experts to five.

## Multi-Expert Routing Impact

Current routing axes (`v0.9.0`-`v0.15.0`, four experts): GAUSS implementation, econometric methodology, software-engineering/code quality, documentation/explanation quality — expressed through a compact activation matrix and common worked patterns rather than a full combinatorial enumeration.

`v0.16.0` adds a fifth axis:

1. GAUSS implementation
2. Econometric methodology
3. Software engineering/code quality
4. Documentation/explanation quality
5. Education/learning-design quality

A fully enumerated five-expert combination table would have 32 rows (2⁵). This plan explicitly does **not** propose enumerating all 32. Instead, `workflows/expert-routing.md`'s eventual five-expert update should:

- Keep the existing routing-pattern approach `v0.15.0` established (compact activation matrix plus worked examples for the combinations most likely in practice, with a general coordination rule covering any combination without its own named row).
- Add Education as a fifth column in the activation matrix.
- Keep explicit, worked examples for the Education-involving combinations most likely to occur in practice: Education-only, GAUSS + Education, Econometrics + Education, Documentation + Education, Software Engineering + Education, GAUSS + Documentation + Education, Econometrics + Documentation + Education, GAUSS + Econometrics + Documentation + Education, all five, and Neither — the ten combinations named in this plan's Benchmark Plan and routing-examples list below.
- Preserve every existing four-expert pattern already documented in `v0.15.0` unchanged, adding Education only where a worked example specifically needs it, rather than rewriting the whole matrix from scratch.
- Preserve Neither-expert routing unchanged for ordinary writing tasks unrelated to technical education, documentation, code, GAUSS, or econometrics.
- This is the same "readable at N combinations, not a combinatorial engine" discipline `v0.14.0` committed to at eight and `v0.15.0` extended to twelve; this plan extends that discipline further rather than abandoning it at thirty-two.

### reasoning.md

Should describe a step-by-step reasoning workflow, following the `experts/documentation/reasoning.md` precedent (a numbered workflow plus special-case sections):

1. Identify the learner and the learning goal.
2. Identify prior knowledge and prerequisites.
3. Identify learning context and time horizon (a single session vs. a multi-week path).
4. Separate education from documentation/reference help — a lesson is not a reference page, and a reference page is not a lesson.
5. Separate learning design from implementation correctness — a well-sequenced lesson is never evidence the GAUSS code inside it is valid.
6. Separate learning design from methodological correctness — a well-sequenced lesson is never evidence the statistical content is valid.
7. Separate learning design from maintainability/design correctness — a well-sequenced lesson about code is never evidence the code itself is well-designed.
8. Define learning objectives before choosing content or sequence.
9. Sequence concepts from prerequisite to dependent, managing cognitive load.
10. Scaffold practice — worked examples before guided practice before independent practice.
11. Design feedback and checks for understanding appropriate to the objective.
12. Coordinate with the GAUSS Language Expert, Econometrics Expert, Software Engineering Expert, and/or Documentation Expert when the content also touches their domains, rather than answering on their behalf.
13. Avoid overclaiming — never let learning-design quality stand in for syntax verification, methodological soundness, maintainability, documentation completeness, or product value.

### best_practices.md

Should include, following the flat-numbered-list precedent:

- Start with the learner outcome, not the content list.
- Identify prerequisites explicitly.
- Sequence from concrete to abstract where useful.
- Scaffold complexity gradually.
- Use worked examples before open-ended practice.
- Include checks for understanding.
- Provide feedback criteria, not just an answer key.
- Design practice that matches the stated objectives.
- Manage cognitive load — introduce one new idea at a time where possible.
- Surface common misconceptions explicitly rather than hoping they don't arise.
- Include a remediation path for learners who don't grasp a concept the first time.
- Distinguish reference docs (Documentation Expert territory) from lessons (this expert's territory).
- Keep exercises realistic but bounded in scope.
- Avoid unsupported syntax or methodology claims inside examples and exercises.
- Label pseudocode clearly.
- Use documentation as supporting material, not a replacement for instruction.

### common_mistakes.md

Should include, following the flat-numbered-list precedent, explicitly naming boundary-crossing mistakes as failure modes:

- Writing a content list instead of a learning sequence.
- Confusing documentation with education.
- Overloading beginners too early.
- Missing prerequisites.
- Vague learning objectives.
- Exercises that do not match the stated objectives.
- No feedback or assessment plan.
- No remediation path.
- Unsupported GAUSS syntax in examples.
- Unsupported statistical claims in lessons.
- Treating a clear explanation as durable learning (a direct restatement of the Documentation/Education boundary above, phrased as a failure mode to avoid).
- Treating product onboarding as the same as education (a direct restatement of the Education/Product boundary above).
- Making lessons too broad to actually teach anything well.
- Failing to address misconceptions.
- Skipping practice in favor of explanation alone.

## Risks and Design Questions

- **How to keep the Education Expert distinct from the Documentation Expert?** Bounded by the Expert Boundary Rules above: documentation helps a reader complete a task or understand a reference right now; education helps a reader learn over time through sequencing, scaffolding, exercises, and pedagogy. Benchmark `23.4` exists specifically to test this boundary.
- **How to keep the Education Expert distinct from the (future) Product Expert?** Bounded by the same boundary rules: education supports learner development; product supports problem framing, workflow fit, adoption, positioning, and prioritization. Benchmark `23.7` exists specifically to test this boundary.
- **How to prevent lesson quality from implying syntax/statistical/design correctness?** Bounded by the same independence principle already established for the Software Engineering and Documentation Experts, extended one more time: a well-sequenced lesson ≠ correct syntax, sound methodology, or maintainable code. Benchmark `23.6` exists specifically to test the syntax case.
- **How to keep routing readable with five operational experts?** By keeping the routing-pattern discipline `v0.15.0` established (compact activation matrix plus worked examples for common combinations, general coordination rule for the rest) rather than enumerating all 32 possible combinations.
- **Should routing enumerate common Education combinations only?** Yes — this plan recommends the ten combinations named above (Education-only through all-five, plus Neither) as the documented set, with the general coordination principle covering any combination without its own row, matching the precedent set for the fourth expert.
- **How should assessments and feedback be represented without becoming LMS implementation?** Keep it at the design level — what to check for, what feedback criteria to use, what a remediation path looks like — rather than specifying a particular assessment platform, quiz format, or grading system. This expert designs learning experiences, not learning-management-system features.
- **How much beginner-friendly explanation belongs to Documentation vs. Education?** Recommended: making a single piece of content beginner-friendly (clearer wording, defined terms, a simpler example) is Documentation Expert territory, already established in `v0.15.0`; sequencing multiple pieces of content into a learning path, with objectives, scaffolded practice, feedback, and pacing, is Education Expert territory. Benchmark `23.4` is the concrete test case for this line.
- **How to avoid turning the Education Expert into generic motivational coaching?** The Non-Activation Cases list is the primary guardrail — a one-off explanation with no teaching-sequence, practice, scaffolding, or progression request stays outside this expert's territory, keeping it scoped to actual learning-design decisions rather than tone or encouragement.
- **Should onboarding be Education, Product, or both?** Recommended: the *learning* content of onboarding (how a new user is taught to use the product effectively) is Education Expert territory; the *product* content of onboarding (what the onboarding flow should prioritize showing, and why) is future Product Expert territory. Both may activate together once Product is operationalized; for now, onboarding-learning-flow requests activate the Education Expert and flag the product-strategy angle as out of scope.
